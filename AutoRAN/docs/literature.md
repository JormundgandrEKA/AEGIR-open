# What the literature says, and what AutoRAN does with it

Read from the PDFs David supplied (3 Nov 2026), not from summaries. Page and table numbers are from those PDFs.

## Sources
- **Kasenda et al., JAMA 2014;311:1045** (1,017 RCT protocols approved 2000–2003 by six ethics committees; follow-up 11.6 years).
- **Briel et al., J Clin Epidemiol 2016;80:8** (172 RCTs reported as stopped for poor recruitment; 131 gave reasons).
- **Williams et al., PLOS ONE 2015** (905 terminated ClinicalTrials.gov trials; read earlier through its PMC page).

## Facts we rely on
| Fact | Source |
|---|---|
| 24.9% of trials were discontinued; poor recruitment was the most frequent reason (9.9% of all trials, 11.2% of patient trials) | Kasenda, Table 2 |

## Where this overrides earlier choices
1. **Several causes are the norm in the literature for recruitment (Briel), but the coded end reason is usually one (Williams, Kasenda).** The stored label stays the set of reasons *stated in the text*. The "primary" is only set when the text says so or a stated cause sits upstream of another. Nothing is guessed to make a story tidier.
2. **Design and recruitment are not rivals.** Briel's largest reason (overly narrow eligibility) is a design decision that causes low enrolment. Both are kept, and the link is recorded only when the wording makes it (`due to`, `resulting in`).
3. **"Business" covers strategy, funding and regulatory-driven administrative stops** (Kasenda's "administrative"), but funding withdrawn *because of* slow recruitment is a recruitment consequence (Briel, funding domain). Keep both labels, with the link.
4. **Harm is rarer than the curated labels suggest** (2.4% of trials; Williams codes risk-benefit as a scientific-data reason). Safety is the class most over-applied in David's review, so it stays under the strictest rules.
5. **Registry text rarely says how or by whom recruitment failed** (Briel). Domain-level risk (below) is therefore a *prior from the literature*, not something read from the trial's own words.

## The risk-domain axis (taxonomy.BRIEL)
Each recruitment sub-label maps to a Briel domain and records whether Briel judged the reason preventable by a pilot. It feeds the future risk model as a prior. It is never written to a trial as a finding. The mapping is ours (Briel did not code registry text) and is flagged as such.

## Limits to keep in mind
- Kasenda's cohort is protocols from 2000–2003 in three countries. The registry population (all of ClinicalTrials.gov, 2005 onward) differs, so these base rates are anchors for plausibility checks, not values to copy.
- Reasons in published reports are the investigators' own account and are subject to their bias and to non-reporting (Briel: 24% gave none).
- Williams and Kasenda used different category schemes. Cross-source comparison is by meaning, not by name.
